# C2. Same variety, new evidence: when a classification changes

*Case material: Lianzhou 廉州 Yue (Hepu, Beihai, Guangxi)*

**Prepared research case.** This is an editorial account of a versioned set of source
statements (bundle iteration 0.2), not a recorded model answer. It concerns **Lianzhou 廉州 in Hepu, Beihai,
Guangxi**. Lianzhou 連州 in northern Guangdong and Lianjiang 廉江 are different
places. The working address in this case is `YUE_QINLIAN`, with locality property
`LIANZHOU_BEIHAI`.

## The question

How can a variety share a criterion associated with Hakka or Gan while being
classified as Yue? What changed in the evidence available to the researcher?

## Reading the source

Yue-Hashimoto explains that Lianzhou and Bobai have aspirated reflexes of earlier
voiced stops and affricates across tonal categories. Under the single criterion
being discussed, this would make them candidates for classification with Hakka
or Gan. This is a statement about historical sound correspondences, not the
presence of voiced consonants in the modern variety. **[K1; printed p.299]**

Later in the chapter, Lianzhou and Shaowu illustrate the limitations of using that
criterion alone. The author reports Lianzhou's earlier identification as Hakka,
then argues for Yue classification when tonal and lexical features are taken
into account. **[K2–K3; printed p.311]**

Footnote 21 connects the earlier identification in Yue-Hashimoto (1970) partly to
the available material: Chao's character-based field notes were designed to
identify phonological correspondences. The author reports that subsequent field
work on colloquial speech confirmed Lianzhou's status as a Yue dialect.
**[K4–K5; printed p.319, note 21]**

These passages support a change in the evidential basis of a classification.
They do not say that the relevant sounds physically changed between 1970 and
1991, nor do they provide a newly calculated classification accuracy score.
The 1970 position is reported retrospectively in the 1991 source; this bundle
does not contain an independently checked copy of the 1970 publication.

## What the graph lets the reader inspect

The core of this iteration contains all **five active statements** in the declared scope:
this source, the Beihai locality, and the five selected feature categories. Each
statement retains its working target, native feature ID and path through
Evidence to Source. The graph has **16 nodes and 20 native relationships**.

The reader can select a feature category, inspect its statements, follow their
evidence, and return to the common working target. Changing the filter changes
the visible statements. This prepared account continues to describe the baseline
case; it is not regenerated by the filter. Empty results mean that this bundle
has no matching records, not that a linguistic feature is absent.

The historical ordering and the roles K1–K5 are editorial annotations grounded
in the quoted passages. The application has not supplied native causal edges or
automatically discovered the historical explanation. Its contribution here is
the inspectable, source-attributed assembly of differently framed statements
about the same addressed variety. This case alone does not demonstrate that
plain-text retrieval could never recover the passages.

## Reading the record critically

The comparison view adds **four Chen–Chen (2005) records**. Three have a native
Lianzhou locality tag: S1 and S2 describe the historical reflex on printed p.59,
while S3 links to the named Lianzhou rows in the comparison tables on pp.94–95.
S1 and S2 share one evidence passage; their different feature categories must
not be treated as independent observations.

S4 is a subgroup-level record with no native locality tag. Its applicability to
Lianzhou is established through the preceding named table and recorded as a
curated annotation. The link between S3 and S4 is a **curated reading of one table
passage, not a graph fact**: their evidence sits in two different chunks with no
overlapping span, so the `shared_basis` operation does not pair them. An explicit
context option adds S4; the native-locality-only view contains eight records, and
the view with that context contains nine.
The two-source graph has 25 nodes and 35 native relationships. Source filtering
returns five records for Yue-Hashimoto and four for Chen–Chen when curated
context is included. These later descriptions support the phonological comparison;
they are not additional eyewitness reports of the 1970 reclassification.

## Native operations

Seven operations run on this slice. Each one is plain Cypher over native fields,
recorded with its parameters and its checked result in
[expected_native_operations.json](expected_native_operations.json); all seven were
re-run against production on 2026-09-09 and returned the recorded rows.

- **Statements resting on the same passage** (`shared_basis`, 2 rows). Two statements
  are treated as one basis when they share an Evidence node, or when their evidence
  spans overlap inside the same chunk. The operation returns exactly the two pairs
  this account warns about in prose: K4/K5 in footnote 21, and S1/S2 on one passage.
  A coarser "same chunk" test would wrongly add K2/K3, whose spans are adjacent but
  do not overlap.
- **How many independent textual bases** (`independent_bases`, 5 rows). Every active
  claim in the graph carries exactly one Evidence, so the meaningful count exists only
  per slice: 9 claims rest on 8 evidence nodes in 6 chunks from 2 sources, and after
  merging the two shared-basis pairs the slice has **7 independent textual supports**.
  Distinct sources are distinct documents, not proven scholarly independence.
- **Which source says what** (`source_split`, 6 rows). Five records for Yue-Hashimoto
  and four for Chen–Chen, broken down by feature category.
- **Two sources side by side** (`source_comparison_by_feature`, 5 rows). One shared
  category (aspiration contrast), three unique to Yue-Hashimoto, one unique to
  Chen–Chen, no contrast row. The shipped comparison template compares two targets;
  this slice has a single target, so the same query shape is used over sources instead.
- **Where the record disagrees with itself** (`contested_cell`, 1 row). The only cell
  with opposite polarity is voiced obstruents. It is a coding artefact rather than a
  scholarly dispute, and must be shown together with the next operation.
- **Polarity against the recorded value** (`polarity_value_consistency`, 9 rows). Two
  records of one source carry the same `present: false` value under opposite polarity.
  This is the native explanation of the contested cell above.
- **Two readings inside one feature** (`feature_level_split`, 15 rows, marked
  **partially native**). Across the wider Qinlian target the `present` flag happens to
  separate the diachronic reflex from the synchronic inventory without exception, but
  that split is observed on 15 records and is not a contract anywhere in the system.
  The distinguishing wording lives in free text. The level of a statement therefore
  stays a curated annotation.

What these operations do **not** supply: the roles K1–K5 and S1–S4, the historical
ordering, the applicability of S4, the corrected printed pages, the semantic level of
a feature, and any judgement about the scholarly independence of two documents. Those
remain curated annotations and are listed as such in the bundle.

Separately, the disambiguation of the three homographic names is itself the result of
curation performed in production, not a property the graph discovered. What the graph
supplies is the record of that curation: each affected statement carries the run
identifier, the mapping rule and its previous locality and target, so the operation can
be shown and audited. See [provenance.json](provenance.json) and
[diff_0.1_to_0.2.json](diff_0.1_to_0.2.json).
